# EXJET search and AI visibility launch playbook

The website now supplies the technical foundation. First-page rankings and recommendation-engine visibility also require authority, verified business data, useful content, and consistent external signals. No ethical vendor can guarantee the number-one position for every aircraft or charter query.

## Launch day

1. Point `exjet.com` and `www.exjet.com` to the production deployment. Select one canonical host and permanently redirect the other.
2. Add the exact production domain to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
3. Submit `https://exjet.com/sitemap.xml` to both services.
4. Inspect and request indexing for the homepage, Charter, Aircraft, Membership, Sales, About, FAQ, and Contact pages.
5. Verify that `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, `manifest.webmanifest`, `llms.txt`, canonical tags, and social preview images resolve on the production domain.
6. Connect privacy-respecting analytics and conversion events for quote starts, quote completions, calls, email clicks, membership inquiries, and sales inquiries.
7. Replace the email-preparation quote flow with the reviewed server-side lead endpoint. Record no sensitive itinerary data in analytics or application logs.

## Local and entity authority

1. Claim or update the EXJET Google Business Profile with the exact name, address, phones, website, hours, categories, and service areas used on the website.
2. Keep the same NAP data across Yelp, LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, aviation directories, chamber listings, and corporate databases.
3. Add original office, team, and aircraft-service photography to the Business Profile.
4. Build a compliant review-request process after completed trips. Never gate or manufacture reviews.
5. Publish executive bios, real aviation credentials, memberships, and speaking or media appearances when available.

## Content clusters

Publish expert pages only when EXJET can provide genuinely useful information. Avoid thin city swaps and doorway pages.

### Charter decision content

- Private jet charter cost: what changes the quote
- Light vs midsize vs super-midsize aircraft
- How to choose an airport for private charter
- Empty legs: flexibility, risk, and confirmation
- Private charter safety records and operator due diligence
- Wi-Fi, baggage, pets, catering, and international permits

### Route and region content

Create strong route guides for real demand pairs, with airport choices, runway constraints, typical category fit, seasonality, and ground-transfer context. Begin with Dallas, New York, Miami, Los Angeles, Las Vegas, Aspen, London, Dubai, and major event traffic that EXJET actually serves.

### Group and cargo authority

- Team and entertainment group-charter planning
- NCAA, professional sports, and event movement checklists
- Aircraft cargo categories and loading constraints
- Urgent, outsize, automotive, medical, and humanitarian cargo planning

### Aircraft sales authority

- Pre-purchase inspection process
- Maintenance-program and engine-program evaluation
- Title, escrow, and closing sequence
- Model-specific market notes using current, supportable data

## AI recommendation visibility

1. Keep public facts clear, consistent, and easy to quote. The `llms.txt` file reinforces the core entity description but does not replace normal search indexing.
2. Publish named-author expert content with dates, sources, revision history, and firsthand operating or transaction insight.
3. Earn mentions and links from credible aviation, business, event, destination, and local publications.
4. Maintain Organization, FAQ, Breadcrumb, and service structured data without marking up claims that are not visible on the page.
5. Answer customer questions in complete language. AI systems favor sources that resolve the question directly and consistently.
6. Build a real press and knowledge footprint outside the EXJET domain. Recommendation engines need corroborating sources, not only self-published claims.

## Performance and quality budget

- Target LCP under 2.5 seconds on mid-tier mobile over 4G.
- Keep CLS below 0.1 and INP below 200 ms.
- Deliver responsive WebP or AVIF aircraft images and avoid loading offscreen interactive media early.
- Preserve 44 px touch targets, keyboard access, visible focus, meaningful alt text, and reduced-motion behavior.
- Test 390 px, 768 px, 1024 px, 1440 px, and 1920 px viewports before every major release.

## Monthly operating rhythm

- Review Search Console indexing, queries, pages, countries, devices, and Core Web Vitals.
- Improve pages with impressions but weak click-through using clearer titles and descriptions.
- Expand pages that already earn qualified inquiries rather than chasing vanity traffic.
- Audit broken links, stale claims, expired sales listings, aircraft availability language, and structured data.
- Publish one substantial expert resource and one proof-based company update every month.
- Track leads, qualified opportunities, and closed revenue by landing page. Traffic without qualified RFQs is not the goal.
