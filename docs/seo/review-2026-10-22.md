# EXJET search optimization review

Reviewed 22 October 2026. Scope: all 227 rendered public pages, their metadata, content structure, crawlable links, images and structured data in the production build. The authenticated workspace remains outside public search and was reviewed through its noindex layout and response-header configuration.

## Outcome

- 215 public pages are indexable in production. The sitemap contains exactly those 215 URLs, without duplicates.
- 12 utility, draft-policy, membership and archived sales-detail pages remain noindexed intentionally.
- 142 page titles and five descriptions were revised, affecting 143 pages. Existing descriptive route and aircraft quote titles were retained where they already matched the page's purpose.
- The homepage and contact metadata now reflect the five services: Empty legs, Charters, Dry lease, Fractional and Ownership.
- Airport titles identify private jet charter intent and airport codes. Model articles emphasize cabin, range and research; aircraft detail pages retain commercial cost-and-charter intent.
- Destination and experience titles now identify travel guides and their specific focus, rather than relying on slogans. They do not promise direct jet access to places that require connections.
- Added contextual links and planning content for executive assistants, family offices, corporate travel, dry lease, fractional ownership and aircraft acquisitions. Updated Ways to Fly to match the five services.
- No meta-keyword tags, hidden keyword blocks, fabricated prices, fake reviews, invented live availability or mass city doorway pages were added.

## Validation

- Production build and photography integrity checks passed.
- All 227 public page URLs returned HTTP 200 from the locally running production build; a deliberately missing URL returned 404.
- No duplicate titles or descriptions among indexable pages.
- One H1 on each indexable page; all indexed pages reachable through rendered links from the homepage.
- No broken internal page targets detected in rendered HTML.
- Production canonicals and Open Graph page URLs use `https://www.exjet.com/` and match the page path.
- Social image hosts and JSON-LD syntax passed the checks; no preview hosts found in structured data.
- Image alt attributes present; decorative images may correctly use empty alt text.
- Production indexing enabled; preview and development indexing disabled, tested independently.
- Avinode code, API behavior, homepage hero image and navigation logo were not changed.

The page-by-page results are in [page-audit-2026-10-22.csv](page-audit-2026-10-22.csv). “Pass” refers to the automated structural checks, not a ranking, Google endorsement or verification of every aviation statement. Main-content word counts are diagnostic; no minimum word count is treated as a ranking requirement.

Re-run the audit after a production-mode build:

```sh
NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL=https://www.exjet.com npm run build
python3 scripts/seo/audit.py --production --csv docs/seo/page-audit-2026-10-22.csv
```

## Measurement still required

Google Search Console indexing/coverage, search impressions, clicks, queries and actual positions were not available in this session. Backlinks, paid-tool keyword volumes and difficulty, field Core Web Vitals and competitor ranking shares were not measured. Live external crawl behavior is distinct from the validated production build. No claim is made that every page has been indexed or that rankings have already improved.

Monitor Search Console after Google recrawls the changed pages, then prioritize queries that gain impressions but have weak click-through or positions. Use actual enquiries and qualified leads alongside search traffic. Keep factual aircraft, airport and travel information reviewed as sources change.

Google says its normal SEO foundations apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode; it does not require a separate AI optimization file or special schema. Neither these changes nor keyword selection guarantees inclusion in Google Search, AI answers or Gemini responses.
